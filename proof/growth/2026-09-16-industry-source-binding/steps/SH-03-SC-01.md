## What this step does and does not do

- Does: names the four delivery states every industry artefact moves through — `draft-export`, `saved-draft`, `reviewed-estimate`, `issued-deliverable` — and fixes the metadata that identifies a deliverable.
- Does: fix the two things that must never happen to a frozen deliverable: its content hash changing, and a superseded deliverable being edited or advanced.
- Does: reuse the alteration issue's supersession shape (`status: active | superseded`, `supersededAt` / `supersededById` / `supersededByRevision`) so the residential issue set and the industry reports speak the same language.
- Does not: wire any worksheet, report export or issue UI to this contract. Those are ROOF-06 / HVAC-06 / QS-05 / QS-06, each with its own step record.
- Does not: claim any report is a verified estimate or issued deliverable. The contract only *describes* states; it does not promote a result into one.

## Rules the tests establish

1. A `draft-export` or `saved-draft` carries no review or issue timestamp, cannot be superseded, and must be `active`.
2. A `reviewed-estimate` records `reviewedAt` and nothing later; an `issued-deliverable` records both `reviewedAt` and `issuedAt`.
3. A `superseded` deliverable must be `issued-deliverable`, must record `supersededAt` and `supersededById`, and an `active` record may carry no supersession pointers at all.
4. A deliverable's `sourceBinding`, when present, must belong to the same project — a report cannot cite another project's source.
5. `advanceDelivery` moves exactly one state forward at a time, so a deliverable can never be issued without first being reviewed, and never regress or skip.
6. Advancing never changes `contentSha256` — asserted on every step, not merely by convention.
7. `supersedeDelivery` marks an active issued deliverable superseded with `supersededAt` / `supersededById` / `supersededByRevision`, and carries `contentSha256`, `sourceBinding` and `revision` through byte-for-byte; it refuses a non-issued, already-superseded, or cross-project target.
8. `assertDeliveryContentIntact` proves frozen content still matches its recorded hash, and refuses a mismatch.
9. Only `reviewed-estimate` and `issued-deliverable` are frozen; the two draft states are not.
10. Strict validation refuses unknown keys, a short hash, surrounding-whitespace identifiers and a wrong `format`.

## Reuse of existing issue infrastructure

The supersession shape is copied from the alteration issue record ([`alterationIssueSchema.ts`](../../../../src/studio/architect/alterationIssueSchema.ts)): `status: current | superseded` becomes `active | superseded`, and the `supersededAt` / `supersededById` / `supersededByRevision` pointers match field-for-field. This contract is the shared vocabulary; the alteration issue stays a residential specialisation with its own frozen `sourceJson` / `sourceSha256`, which `checkedAlterationIssue` already verifies. Nothing in the residential issue path is changed by this step.

## Remaining limits

- No worksheet, report export, or issue UI consumes this contract yet; it is not reachable from the browser. The consumers are ROOF-06, HVAC-06, QS-05 and QS-06.
- The contract stores `contentSha256` but not the frozen bytes; the industry-specific wrapper stores the content and supplies the hash (exactly as the alteration issue stores `sourceJson`). `assertDeliveryContentIntact` is the bridge.
- Revisions are positive integers; the contract does not itself enforce that consecutive revisions of one `projectId` + `kind` form a gapless sequence — callers (as in `appendAlterationIssue`) own that check.
- No industry-readiness, verified-estimate, quote-eligibility or native/desktop acceptance is claimed.
